WATTSENSE: TARGETED LITERATURE CHECK
Reviewed 30 September 2026

Targeted primary-literature check for a design-and-simulation case study; not a systematic review or exhaustive novelty search.
Inspected primary publisher records and available author/institution-hosted papers through web retrieval. Search snippets alone were not used to support detailed method claims. No manuscript was uploaded to an external service.

MOVASSAGH2021
Kiarash Movassagh, Arif Raihan, Balakumar Balasingam, Krishna Pattipati
A Critical Look at Coulomb Counting Approach for State of Charge Estimation in Batteries
Energies, 2021, vol. 14, no. 14, article 4074
DOI: 10.3390/en14144074
Primary URL: https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/14/14/4074
Inspected copy/record: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220008848
Full text: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220008848/downloads/2021.07%20-%20Energies%20-%20A%20Critical%20Look%20at%20Coulomb%20Counting%20Approach%20for%20State%20of%20Charge%20Estimation.pdf
Evidence inspected: Publisher citation record and NASA-hosted accepted manuscript, including abstract, inspected. NASA identifies the manuscript as externally peer reviewed and records publication on 6 July 2021. Later PDF section requests intermittently returned retrieval errors.
Supported claim: Coulomb-counting errors can arise from current measurement, numerical integration, capacity uncertainty and timing; their accumulation requires attention to calibration and initialization.
Manuscript use: Motivate the current/calibration and known-capacity limitations, while citing the established method rather than claiming it is new.
Limit: Does not validate Wattsense, its sensor settings, its full-charge procedure, or any particular percentage SOC accuracy. The arXiv precursor has a different title and author-name variant; use the final journal metadata above.

MARAHATTA2021
Anup Marahatta, Yaju Rajbhandari, Ashish Shrestha, Ajay Singh, Anand Gachhadar, Anup Thapa
Priority-based low voltage DC microgrid system for rural electrification
Energy Reports, 2021, vol. 7, pp. 43-51
DOI: 10.1016/j.egyr.2020.11.030
Primary URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352484720314554
Inspected copy/record: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/346036129_Priority-based_low_voltage_DC_microgrid_system_for_rural_electrification
Evidence inspected: Publisher abstract and citation metadata retrieved. Direct publisher open failed, so the author-uploaded full paper (Ashish Shrestha, uploaded 20 November 2020) was inspected, especially section 2.2.2 and Table 3. Author byline matches the article title page.
Supported claim: Earlier work combines SCADA with priority-based control in a DC microgrid, including three load levels and battery-charge thresholds for staged disconnection.
Manuscript use: Closest practical prior art; establishes that maintaining essential-load service through lower-priority shedding is an existing concept.
Limit: Its multi-level solar/microgrid experiment is not directly comparable to the two-load Wattsense discharge scenario. Its claimed reliability results must not be transferred to Wattsense.

RAJBHANDARI2024
Yaju Rajbhandari, Anup Marahatta, Ashish Shrestha, Anand Gachhadar, Anup Thapa, Francisco Gonzalez-Longatt, Petr Korba
Enhanced demand side management for solar-based isolated microgrid system: Load prioritisation and energy optimisation
IET Smart Grid, 2024, vol. 7, no. 3, pp. 294-313
DOI: 10.1049/stg2.12151
Primary URL: https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/stg2.12151
Evidence inspected: Publisher full text inspected: abstract, sections 2.1-2.2, author metadata, and issue citation. First online 18 December 2023; cite the June 2024 volume/issue publication year.
Supported claim: Priority-based demand management has also been formulated using day-ahead generation/load forecasts and particle-swarm optimization to allocate energy across load groups.
Manuscript use: Position Wattsense as a local deterministic threshold case study with no forecasting or optimization; the difference is scope and implementation, not demonstrated superiority.
Limit: No head-to-head comparison has been conducted. Do not quote its load-satisfaction or continuity percentages as evidence of Wattsense performance.

ZHANG2018
Cheng Zhang, Walid Allafi, Quang Dinh, Pedro Ascencio, James Marco
Online estimation of battery equivalent circuit model parameters and state of charge using decoupled least squares technique
Energy, 2018, vol. 142, pp. 678-688
DOI: 10.1016/j.energy.2017.10.043
Primary URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360544217317127
Inspected copy/record: https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/94268/
Full text: https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/94268/9/WRAP-online-estimation-battery-Marco-2017.pdf
Evidence inspected: University repository published-version PDF inspected: title page, abstract, introduction, experimental section 4.1 and discussion 6.1. Names follow the article byline; repository expands Dinh and Ascencio differently.
Supported claim: Battery equivalent-circuit parameters depend on operating conditions; this study estimates fast and slow dynamics separately and tests the method with lithium-ion cell measurements.
Manuscript use: Explain why Wattsense's assumed OCV/resistance plant is only a design-screening model and requires pack-specific characterization before predictive accuracy claims.
Limit: The published experimental accuracy and cell parameters do not apply to the unmeasured Wattsense LiFePO4 pack; no DWRLS estimator is implemented in Wattsense.

SYNTHESIS
The established components are Coulomb counting, priority shedding, telemetry and equivalent-circuit modeling. A defensible contribution is reproducible integration of firmware checks, PCB-path assumptions and explicit service/startup tradeoffs. No novel control algorithm or hardware validation is established.
All claims above are short original paraphrases. Do not copy external figures. Use original project diagrams and cite prior concepts. Keep the final paper's source-derived discussion concise; retrieval sources carried 200-word per-source response budgets.
